About us

Sixty-two years of keeping the light on

Beacon began as a single reading room donated by a retired harbourmaster. Today it's a full public library, community archive, and gathering place for Harbour Ward.

Our mission

Free access, for everyone, for good

Beacon exists so that anyone in Harbour Ward — student, sailor, retiree, newcomer — has a warm, well-lit place to read, learn, and be part of something. Membership is free. Every program is free. We believe a library card should never be a luxury item.

Beyond lending books, we preserve the ward's own story: shipping ledgers, oral histories from the old fish market, and photographs going back to the 1930s, all catalogued and open to the public.

Open to all

No fees, no residency paperwork beyond one proof of address. Every program is free to attend.

Locally rooted

A third of our budget goes to preserving Harbour Ward's own history — maps, records, and voices.

Quietly modern

Free wifi, a digital lending shelf, and a printer that actually works. Old values, current tools.

History

How we got here

1962

A harbourmaster's reading room

Captain E. Ferreira donates his personal collection of 900 volumes and a room above the old harbour office — Beacon's first home.

1978

The ward petitions for a proper building

Rapid local growth outgrows the reading room. Residents raise funds for the current site on Harbour Light Road.

1994

Local Heritage Archive founded

A dedicated wing begins collecting shipping ledgers, fishing-guild records, and oral histories from ward elders.

2011

Children's wing opens

A sun-lit reading room and the ward's first free after-school storytime program.

2023

Digital lending shelf launches

E-books and audiobooks become available to every member, free of charge, from any device.

Special collections

The Harbour Ward Local Heritage Archive

Housed on the third floor, the archive holds items you won't find catalogued anywhere else — open to any visitor, no appointment required for general browsing.

Shipping ledgers, 1889–1965

Cargo and crew records from the old harbour office, largely unpublished elsewhere.

Oral history recordings

Over 200 recorded interviews with ward elders, fishing-guild members, and dockworkers.

Photograph collection

Roughly 4,000 prints and negatives documenting the ward from the 1930s onward.

Come see it for yourself

The reading room is open seven days a week. No appointment needed for a first visit.
